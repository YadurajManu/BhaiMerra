# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue with the code, not the person who wrote it.

## What that means here

Fleet OS is a small project and most of its conversations are technical
disagreements — about a scheduling decision, an error message, whether a
default is wrong. Those are welcome and the project is better for them. What
is not welcome is anything that makes people not want to have them:

- Personal attacks, insults, or dismissing someone rather than their argument.
- Harassment of any kind, public or private. Sexualised language or imagery.
- Comments demeaning someone for who they are — including their age,
  disability, appearance, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, level of
  experience, nationality, race, religion, or sexual orientation.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Persistently derailing a thread after being asked to stop.

Two specific things, because this is an infrastructure project and both come up:

**Nobody is stupid for not knowing something.** People arrive here having
never run a container. "Read the docs" is not an answer; if the docs did not
answer it, that is a documentation bug and worth more than the question.

**Strong technical opinions are fine. Contempt is not.** "This design is wrong
because X" is a contribution. "Only an idiot would design it this way" is not
the same sentence with more energy — it is a worse one, because it invites a
reply about the person instead of about X.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, discussions, and
anywhere someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Email **hello@fleet-os.dev**.

Being honest about what that means: reports go to one person, who is also the
maintainer of the project you may be reporting about. There is no committee and
no appeals process. If that makes you uncomfortable reporting something —
which is reasonable — GitHub's own
[abuse reporting](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam)
routes to people who have no stake in this project at all.

I will keep reports confidential, and I will not discuss them with the person
reported without asking you first.

## Enforcement

Depending on what happened: a private word, a public correction, having a
comment removed, or being blocked from the repository. I would rather use the
first of those and usually will. Repeated or severe behaviour goes straight to
the last one.

I hold myself to this too. If I am the problem, the GitHub route above exists
precisely for that.
